Principles

Micro-apps, made properly.

Windfall apps are small, built for a niche, and made to the standard you’d expect from a serious software company. These are the rules every app follows, and the honest version of how free works.

For one person

Personal apps

What you can make yourself with an AI chat in an afternoon.

  • Good enough for its maker
  • Security and privacy are an afterthought
  • Nobody else relies on it
For a niche · Windfall lives here

Micro-apps

One focused job for a small group of people, built to an enterprise quality bar.

  • Security review, tests and release checks
  • Privacy, licensing and accessibility handled
  • Updates that don’t break what you rely on
For everyone

Enterprise apps

Huge products with huge teams.

  • High quality bar
  • Built for the broad middle
  • Your niche rarely makes the roadmap

How they’re made

Built for real needs

Micro-apps exist to solve a real problem for real people. Every app here so far is one we use almost every day, so it gets tested hard and improved constantly. Apps grown from your requests start with your needs instead of ours. If we don’t use an app ourselves, your feedback decides whether it keeps going.

The same bar as hand-made software

Every app gets the checks a professional team would run: security, privacy, compliance, licensing, accessibility, tests and a release process. That’s why a Windfall app isn’t the same as asking a chatbot to write one.

Built by AI, answerable to people

AI agents write, test and release the code, and a person is responsible for every app. When something goes wrong, a person answers for it. AI features inside an app are labelled as AI.

Your data

Your data is yours

Wherever an app can work that way, your data lives on your device, you can export it, and you can delete it. Some apps can’t work fully like this, and their page says so.

No tracking by default

Nothing about you or how you use an app is sent anywhere unless you turn it on. Usage sharing is opt-in, and you can turn it off again.

What you share is what you get

When sharing powers a feature, the feature goes to the people who share. When sharing only helps make the app better, everyone gets the better app.

Share your workouts to see how you compare with others: only people who share see the comparison.
Share crash reports: everyone gets the fixes.

What apps cost

Free to you isn’t free to make. Every app has four kinds of cost.

BuildingResearch, design and the factory time to make it. Paid once, up front.
UpkeepSecurity fixes, compliance and updates that keep it safe. Never stops, even when nothing new is added.
RunningHosting, storage and sync. Grows with the number of people using it.
Per useThings that cost money every time they happen, like an AI request.

Today we cover building, upkeep and running ourselves. These are apps we and our families use, and at our size they fit inside free hosting plans. Per-use costs are what Premium features are for.

The freemium deal

You get
  • Apps that are free to use today
  • Premium marked up front, wherever we already know a feature costs money per use
  • No tracking by default
  • Open costs and modest prices
  • A clear reason, and a say in what comes next, when something free changes
  • Notice and your data if an app is retired
You give

Feedback. That’s the price of a free app.

Send it yourself (tell us what works and what doesn’t), and/or turn on usage sharing: crash reports, what fails, and where you spend your time. Sharing is off until you choose it. If nobody shares anything, an app can only improve by guesswork, so turning it on, or writing in, is how you help the app you use get better.

Feedback also keeps an app alive. We keep the apps we use ourselves up to date anyway. Other apps get upkeep while people tell us they matter.

Premium is marked up front

When we already know a feature costs us money each time it’s used, such as AI scanning with your camera, the app labels it Premium from day one, even while it’s free. A price for it later won’t be a surprise.

Prices are open and modest

AI makes software far cheaper to build, and those savings should reach you. We do want to make a living from this one day, so prices include a margin, but a small one, and we’ll be open about what things cost us.

When costs change, we’ll tell you why

Some costs aren’t in our control. An app can outgrow a free hosting plan overnight, or a provider can raise its prices. Sometimes we’ll have to make a quick change just to keep an app running, such as a usage limit or a temporary Premium tier. Whenever that happens, we’ll explain what changed and what it costs, and open the discussion with the people using the app about where it goes next. The longer-term options are a Premium tier, a small subscription, ads, or retiring the app, and your feedback shapes which.

If an app costs more to keep safe than it gives back and nobody wants to pay for it, we’ll retire it properly rather than let it go stale and put you at risk.

No app disappears overnight. If one is retired, you get notice ahead of time and a way to take your data with you.

How does this pay for itself?

Right now, we pay for it. Here’s what happens as it grows.

Why are the apps free right now?
  • They’re cheap to run at today’s size. The apps run on free hosting plans, and we cover building and upkeep because we and our families use them.
  • Feedback is the payment. What you tell us, and what you choose to share, makes the apps better and teaches the factory to build the next one.
What if an app gets popular?
  • Then it starts to cost money. A spelling game with a million players needs paid servers. We may have to make a quick change to keep it running, then we’ll open the discussion with its users about how it earns its keep from there: Premium, a subscription or ads.
Will anything else cost money?
  • In Windfall, Premium features. These are things that cost money per use, like AI.
  • Outside Windfall, yes. Windfall is the freemium part of CanopyPowered, the family of apps grown in the Orchard. Larger, specialised apps, such as software for a professional practice, will be paid, and one day a single subscription may cover several apps and their Premium features.
Can I support the work?
  • Yes, thank you. You can sponsor us directly. It goes towards the factory and the upkeep that keeps every app safe. Apps don’t need per-app subscriptions today.
